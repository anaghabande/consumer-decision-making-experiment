# Consumer Decision Making Experimental Research

## Overview

This repository presents an experimental consumer behavior research project examining how product evaluation context and extrinsic marketing cues influence consumer willingness to pay.

The project combines behavioral economics, consumer psychology, experimental design and statistical analysis to investigate how customers evaluate products under different decision environments.

## Research Question

Why can the same product appear more or less valuable depending on whether consumers evaluate it independently or alongside a competing product?

The project also examines whether an extrinsic cue, specifically a celebrity association, can influence willingness to pay during side by side product comparison.

## Study 1: Evaluability & Preference Reversal

The first experiment replicated Hsee's Evaluability Hypothesis using fictional Bluetooth speakers.

Two product attributes were manipulated:

**Star Rating**

An easy to evaluate attribute because consumers are familiar with rating scales.

**Distortion**

A relatively difficult to evaluate technical attribute because consumers may lack an established reference point.

The experiment tested whether evaluation context could produce a preference reversal between the products.

## Study 2: Celebrity Cue Extension

The second experiment extended the original framework by introducing an extrinsic celebrity association.

The experiment tested whether a celebrity cue could influence willingness to pay for a technically weaker product during direct comparison.

The extension incorporated concepts from:

• Evaluability Hypothesis  
• Cue Utilization Theory  
• Elaboration Likelihood Model  
• Peripheral Cues  
• Celebrity Endorsement  
• Reference Point Effects  

## Research Design

**Total Sample:** 111 respondents

**Geographic Coverage:** 9 countries

**Data Collection:** Google Forms

**Experimental Design:** Between subjects

**Analysis Software:** IBM SPSS

Participants were assigned to different experimental conditions involving separate product evaluation, joint product evaluation and joint evaluation with an extrinsic celebrity cue.

## Statistical Analysis

The project applied quantitative techniques including:

• Independent Samples T Tests  
• Paired Comparisons  
• Manipulation Checks  
• Mean Comparison  
• Statistical Significance Testing  
• Robustness Checks  
• Choice Share Analysis  
• Control Variables  
• Sensitivity Analysis  

## Key Findings

The manipulation check confirmed that respondents found star ratings significantly easier to evaluate than distortion.

However, the predicted preference reversal was not observed.

In the celebrity extension, willingness to pay increased for both products rather than creating a product specific advantage for the endorsed speaker.

Neither primary hypothesis was statistically supported.

Rather than treating null results as failure, the project investigated alternative explanations including sample composition, statistical power, experimental stimuli, currency familiarity and survey context.

## Managerial Implications

The research provides several implications for marketing managers.

### Design the Comparison Environment

Brands with technical advantages can make those attributes easier for customers to evaluate through comparison tables, benchmarks and contextual information.

### Manage Easy to Evaluate Signals

Ratings, endorsements, certifications and other recognizable cues can influence product evaluation when technical information is difficult to interpret.

### Measure Relative Performance

Marketing interventions should be evaluated against competitors rather than relying only on changes in absolute willingness to pay.

### Test Before Scaling

Consumer responses to endorsements and product comparison environments should be experimentally tested before large marketing investments are made.
